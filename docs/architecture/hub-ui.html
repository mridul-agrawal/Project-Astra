5 · What this actually says

  • The hub UI code mostly does follow your standard. HubHUDController is a real composition root: it owns the views, news up the controllers, and has one paired subscribe/unsubscribe. The prompt is a textbook trio.
  • The markers are the one genuine break. HubMarkerManager sits in Core/Hub, holds a UI view directly, solves screen positions, and renders from its own LateUpdate — gameplay driving presentation, outside the pattern and outside the root.
  • The objective panel is now literally the battle map's. Same View, same Model, same hierarchy copied into the hub scene, same hold-O. The hub supplies a Controller that fills the checklist from the visit's quest, pins the panel to the top right, and switches the WIN/LOSE pair off — a visit has stages, not conditions.
  • The root has to hunt for the player. FindFirstObjectByType<HubPlayerController>(), tried from both OnEnable and Start, because she is built mid-Start by the bootstrapper rather than being in the scene.
  • The real gap is coverage, not structure. Four surfaces exist: a verb prompt, the objective panel, off-screen arrows, and the dialogue system's choice menu. Everything else the GDD asks of a hub has no UI at all yet.
  • The other three are generated. HubHudBuilder builds them in code, so anything styled by hand on those objects is lost the next time it runs. The objective panel is exempt: it is copied from BattleMap.unity by its own menu item, which is also how a restyle of the battle one reaches the hub.
