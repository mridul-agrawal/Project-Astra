Against what is in the game today

Verdict

Better, and not marginally. It fixes four of the structural faults in what is there now: the enum-and-switch condition, the effect class with five kinds and blank fields, the runner that both judges and mutates, and the state object doing four unrelated jobs. It also replaces two ragged seams with two named ones.

The test it passes that the current design fails: a new way to finish an objective is a class, and a new thing an objective can do is a class. Neither requires touching an enum, a switch, or a listener.

It is also readable, which was the complaint. Every box is one noun with one job.

What I would raise before building

  1. Manager and Runner have no boundary yet. The board gives the Runner a job and the Manager none. Without a line drawn, the Manager becomes the next wiring closet. The obvious split: Manager is the MonoBehaviour — lifetime, subscriptions, the catalog — and the Runner holds every decision, in plain C#.
  2. Kill, Collect and Give Item are not hub concepts. The hub GDD rules out hub combat and item collection (§2.2) and inventory (§12). Either this system is meant to serve battle-map objectives too, which cuts against keeping the two separate, or those three are placeholders. Worth settling before the shape hardens.
  3. Talk Condition is keyed on UnitId. The requirement credits a conversation completing, and a multi-character conversation credits an authored list rather than one per portrait. A conversation id may be the truer key.
  4. Five requirements have nowhere to live yet: gating (can she enter this building), the marker's source of truth (which targets are still outstanding), save/load, per-objective activation conditions, and author-time validation. These are the ones most likely to bend the shape later, so they are worth placing now rather than bolting on.
  5. Nothing owns the order. Quest Data holds all its objectives, but no box says who keeps the index, or enforces one ACTIVE stage and no going back. Probably QuestProgress — but it should be said.
  6. Quest Event hangs free on the board. I have drawn it under Quest Objective, since the note reads “run on start or completion”. Confirm whether events are authored on a quest, on an objective, or both.
