Proposed Interaction Architecture

Project Astra · hub interaction, designed from the GDD requirements and your whiteboard. Nothing here is anchored to the current code.

The shape

Why this shape

The interface is the whole idea. The controller knows one type, IInteractable, and never asks what kind of thing it is holding. A door, a student, a noticeboard and the departure point are four classes that answer the same five questions differently.

Adding a kind means adding a class. Nothing else is edited — no enum, no gather step, no switch in a director. That is the test this design has to pass, and the reason to prefer it.

Availability is asked, not computed. IProgressionQuery is declared by the interaction system and implemented by the quest system, so a door knows it is locked without anything in here knowing what an objective is. This is the part of your sketch that matters most for keeping the systems separable.

One refinement to the sketch

Your board has a single CanPlayerInteract(). The requirements need that split in two, because they pull in opposite directions:

§4.3 — a character with no conversation shows no prompt at all.
§6.4 — a locked door still prompts, and the press plays its denial line, because "an invisible wall is never the only explanation".

So availability decides whether something is a target; locked is an outcome that Interact() handles. One returns false and disappears; the other returns true and says no in words. Collapsing them loses one of the two rules.

What this costs

Doors stop being data and become objects. Today a door is a struct in the room's asset; here it is a component spawned with the room. That is the change that makes the polymorphism work, and it is the largest single piece of the migration.

Three current classes dissolve. The central resolver's kind-switch becomes each class's own Priority; the director's branch becomes each class's own Interact; the candidate-gathering step becomes registration.

One new seam to agree with the quest system. IProgressionQuery has to be implemented by whatever owns objectives and flags. Worth settling its exact members before any of this is written.
